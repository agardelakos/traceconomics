## What this is

An independent project that follows the economy, run by one person, with no institution or funder behind it. **Every number is computed from public data, and the method, the code and the source files are open, so anyone can check or rebuild them.**

We start with how inflation differs between groups of households in Greece; more topics and countries will follow. The figures are updated each month, after ELSTAT publishes the consumer price index.

## Who it is for

Anyone curious about the economy, with no expertise needed: every figure comes with a plain explanation of what it means and how it was made.

And journalists, analysts, teachers and students, unions and consumer groups: anyone who needs to say, with numbers they can cite, what the official statistics show and what lies behind them.

## What it isn't

Not an official statistic: the statistics offices and Eurostat publish the official figures, and bear no responsibility for these. Our own estimates are labelled as such, each with its method. There are no accounts, and the data are never sold. If you pick a light or dark theme, that one choice is kept in your browser so it holds from page to page; it is never sent anywhere. We count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, from the company that serves this site: which pages are viewed, the site a reader came from, and the country, device and browser, in totals only. It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser and keeps no IP address.

## Take part

The code, the method and the data pipeline are on GitHub: [traceconomics](https://github.com/agardelakos/traceconomics). Ideas, questions and corrections are welcome there.

If the site is useful to you, you can [support it on Buy Me a Coffee](https://buymeacoffee.com/mitossoftwaresolutions). It helps pay for the domain and the time it takes; everything here stays free either way. Support buys no say in the numbers or the method.
