ELSTAT publishes the consumer price index's overall level in two tables: table 05 (the index and its yearly rate since 1959) and table 07 (every item's index, one file a year). We compute the yearly rate from table 07 and compare it with table 05's. They agree exactly in 2011–2013 and from 2025, and differ slightly in between.

## What happened

- **Before February 2026**, every version of table 07 we found has the same overall index as table 05, to the last digit.
- **In February 2026** ELSTAT re-issued all of table 07, from 2010 on, in the new COICOP 2018 classification. Its note of 10 February 2026 says the all-items index stays unchanged "up to the second decimal place".
- **In the re-issued files** the overall index for 2014–2023 differs from table 05 beyond the second decimal, by at most 0.0034 index points. The 2024 file uses the re-issued December 2023 as its starting point. Table 05 was not re-issued: it keeps the index as first published.

## September 2015

One month differs more: September 2015, by 0.13 points in the yearly rate (and September 2016, which compares with it). In the re-issue, the clothing group's September 2015 rose like men's and women's clothing (about 50% from August, after the summer sales); as first published, children's clothing had risen only 22%. COICOP 2018 counts older children's clothes with men's and women's.

## What isn't known

Why the re-issue changed September 2015's clothing: a correction of the first figure, or a by-product of rebuilding the groups. The files show the change, not its reason.

## How common this is

58 of the 189 months from 2011 to September 2026 agree exactly. 129 differ by less than 0.005 points, and the two Septembers by 0.13.
