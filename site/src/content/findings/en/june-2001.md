In the current table 05 the consumer price index for June 2001 rose by **3.9332%** on a year before, but the same file’s index levels give **3.9239%**. The difference is 0.009 percentage points: both show as 3.9% at one decimal.

## What happened

- **July 2001.** The rate was first published as 3.9%, with the index at 107.1 against 103.1 a year before (base 1999 = 100).
- **2010 to 2021.** In every copy of table 05 we found, June 2001’s rate equals its own index ratio to 15 digits.
- **Rebased to 2020 = 100, between July 2021 and November 2022.** Only one month’s level moved beyond the rescaling: June 2000, up by 0.009%. June 2000’s own rate was updated, from 2.5184% to 2.5276%. June 2001’s rate, which compares with June 2000, was not.
- **Since then.** Every month is unchanged in every file up to today’s.

## What isn’t known

Which June 2000 level is right. The rebase may have corrected an old error or brought in a new one: both round to the 103.1 published in 2001.

## How common this is

793 of the table’s 801 rates equal their index ratio exactly. The seven others, June to December 1999, are published rounded to one decimal.
