The survey's public file holds ELSTAT's own household type for every household (one adult, two adults, more, each with or without dependent children; the same as Eurostat's published table). We build the six types ourselves from each person's relationship and activity, by Eurostat's written rule: a dependent child is under 16, or 16–24, a child of the reference person or partner, and neither working nor unemployed. Then we compare each type's share of households, year by year.

## What we found

- **In most years the two agree closely.** They place 95.2% to 99.4% of households alike (2023 aside), and the largest gap in a type's share is 0.1 points in 2008 and 0.5 to 1.4 points from 2015 on.
- **In 2009–2014 some shares differ by 1.9 to 2.9 points**, above the one point we count as agreeing. The differences are mostly between neighbouring types: a 16–24-year-old counted as an adult by one side and as a dependent child by the other.
- **In 2023 ELSTAT's type can't be compared.** Only 34.4% of its "one adult" households have one member, against 100% in 2022 and 2024. Our types agree with it on 26.9% of households.

## What we tried

- **Other rules for a dependent child**, scored against ELSTAT's type over all years: any relative instead of a child of the reference person (96.0% alike), students only (96.9%), any child of the reference person whatever their activity (97.5%; closer than the written rule in 2011–2014), no 16–24 rule at all (89.4%). None reaches 100%.
- **For 2023, the link between households and their members.** In 2023 every household's count of members in the file equals its recorded size, so the members are linked correctly; the mismatch is in the type variable itself.

## What is still open

Why no rule reproduces ELSTAT's type exactly is not known. ELSTAT may use information the public file doesn't carry, or in 2011–2014 a rule that ignores activity; neither is confirmed. Why the 2023 variable doesn't match the household sizes is not known either. A description of ELSTAT's own rule, or a corrected 2023 file, would settle both. Our household types follow Eurostat's written rule in every year, so the figures on this site don't depend on either answer.
